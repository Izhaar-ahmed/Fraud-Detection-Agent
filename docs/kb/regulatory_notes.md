# Regulatory reference notes (summaries written for this project; see the linked source documents)

## FinCEN SAR narrative guidance: five Ws and how
Source: FinCEN, Guidance on Preparing a Complete and Sufficient Suspicious Activity Report Narrative (2003) and SAR Narrative Guidance. A narrative should state who is conducting the suspicious activity, what instruments or mechanisms were used, when it occurred, where it took place, why it is suspicious, and how it was carried out. It should be chronological, concise, and stand on its own without attachments. Include account and transaction identifiers, amounts and dates.

## FinCEN SAR supporting documentation (FIN-2007-G003)
Source: FinCEN FIN-2007-G003. Supporting documentation is retained by the filer and made available to law enforcement on request; it is not attached to the report. The case file behind a report must identify the transactions and evidence relied on.

## FinCEN advisory on account takeover (FIN-2011-A016)
Source: FinCEN Advisory FIN-2011-A016. Red flags: changes in device or login characteristics, access from unfamiliar devices or anonymising proxies, activity inconsistent with customer history, and use of both credentials and card data. Institutions should report account takeover activity as suspicious when detected.

## FinCEN identity-related suspicious activity (2021 financial trend analysis)
Source: FinCEN Financial Trend Analysis, Identity-Related Suspicious Activity (2021). Impersonation and compromised credentials are the most common identity typologies; device and email anomalies are frequent indicators.

## FATF cyber-enabled fraud
Source: FATF, Illicit Financial Flows from Cyber-Enabled Fraud (2023). Organised groups reuse infrastructure such as devices, accounts and email addresses across many victims, and split amounts to stay under monitoring thresholds. Linking shared infrastructure across cases is a key detection method.

## FFIEC suspicious activity reporting
Source: FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual, Suspicious Activity Reporting. Banks file a SAR for known or suspected criminal violations meeting thresholds, within 30 days of initial detection, and maintain documentation supporting the decision to file or not file.

## FFIEC red flags
Source: FFIEC BSA/AML Manual Appendix F. Transactions structured to avoid thresholds, repeated just-below-threshold amounts, and activity inconsistent with the customer's profile are red flags.
